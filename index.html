    Ethical Dilemma

    Insider Misuse of Administrative Privileges

    One ethical issue that has influenced my thinking is the Edward Snowden case. Snowden had authorized access to classified government systems while working as a contractor for the National Security Agency (NSA). Instead of using that access only for his assigned duties, he copied thousands of classified documents and released them to journalists. His actions led to worldwide discussions about privacy, government surveillance, and national security.

    This case taught me that Information Technology professionals have a responsibility to use their access honestly and responsibly. Even when someone has permission to access confidential information, that does not mean they have the right to misuse it. Employers trust IT professionals to protect sensitive information, respect privacy, and follow professional and ethical standards.

    If I were faced with a similar ethical concern, I would follow the organization's reporting procedures instead of sharing confidential information publicly. I would report my concerns to supervisors, ethics officers, or appropriate legal authorities while respecting confidentiality. This approach protects both the organization and the public while maintaining professional integrity.

    My faith teaches me to be honest, trustworthy, and accountable in everything I do. These principles guide the way I want to use technology throughout my career and remind me that technical ability should always be combined with ethical responsibility.

    Ethical Dilemma

    Alternative Solution

    Instead of copying and releasing classified information as Edward Snowden did, a better approach would have been to report his concerns through approved internal whistleblower channels or government oversight organizations.

    This alternative follows important ethical principles including honesty, responsibility, accountability, respect for confidentiality, and professional integrity.

    • Honesty
    • Responsibility
    • Accountability
    • Respect for Confidentiality
    • Professional Integrity

    If internal reporting failed, he could have sought legal protection through authorized whistleblower procedures instead of publicly releasing classified documents. This approach would have protected national security while still allowing legitimate concerns about privacy and government surveillance to be investigated.

    Although the public debate that followed the leaks raised important questions, releasing classified information without authorization violated professional responsibilities and damaged trust between employees and employers (Greenwald, 2014). As a future Cloud Solutions Engineer, I believe professionals should always protect confidential information and use ethical and legal methods to report concerns.
